Before you start

How to use this handbook

You are the subject expert and the author of your chart. CER reviews and adjusts before anything goes on unctad.org – this handbook exists so that review has as little to do as possible.

Each part below covers one thing a reviewer checks, in the order they check it: is the data itself clean, is the chart type right for the question being asked, are the colours doing a job, does the title state a finding, is every field's text in the house style, and are the axes/layout settings the standard web ones. Part 9 collects the corrections CER makes most often – if you only read one part before submitting, read that one.

The one rule that matters most: a chart makes one point. If you can't say the point in one sentence, that sentence is missing – and it's your title.

This handbook covers what a tool can check and what a person has to judge. It doesn't replace review, and it can't cover every case your data might throw at it – when something here doesn't fit, ask CER rather than forcing it.

Before you submit

Pre-submission checklist

Run through this once before handing a chart to CER. Every item is explained in the part referenced.

  • Numbers are numbers, not text with units/commas baked in (Part 1)
  • No blank rows, no "n/a"/".." placeholders left in numeric cells (Part 1)
  • No category value contains "<" or ">" (Part 1)
  • The chart type fits the question, not just the data's shape (Part 2)
  • Four coloured series or fewer where possible; never more than seven (Part 3)
  • Colours are assigned in the UNCTAD order, not picked by eye (Part 3)
  • Title is a finding, phrased as a sentence with a verb (Part 4)
  • Title isn't "Figure N"/"Chart N" – that numbering doesn't travel to the web (Part 4)
  • Description reads: what / breakdown / unit / period (Part 5)
  • Source starts with "UN Trade and Development (UNCTAD)" (Part 6)
  • Bars/columns start at zero, or the exception is stated in the notes (Part 7)
  • Never two vertical axes on one chart (Part 7)
  • Axis labels are on automatic, not hand-set custom ticks (Part 7)
  • Theme, locale, logo and sharing settings are the standard web ones (Part 8)
  • No em dash anywhere – en dash only, everywhere, every field
  • Not a Datawrapper map – UNCTAD doesn't publish those
Part 1

Data prep

Most chart problems are data problems wearing a chart's clothes.

  • Numbers stay numbers. 1,234 or 1 234.5 typed into a cell reads as text, not a number, in Datawrapper – it won't plot, sort, or format correctly. Let Datawrapper's own number formatting add thousands separators at render time.
  • No units inside number cells. 5% or 120 kg in a cell is text. Put the unit in the column header or the chart's description, and leave the cell as a plain number.
  • Leave missing data empty, not n/a, .., or -. Datawrapper treats an empty cell as "no data" and a text placeholder as, again, text that breaks the series.
  • Percentages are fed as 0–100, not 0–1. Datawrapper's 0% number format appends the percent sign without multiplying – 0.85 renders as 0; 85 renders as 85%. This is the opposite of a spreadsheet formula and is easy to get backwards.
  • No category value may contain "<" or ">". A label like Limited reported trade (<$100k) silently fails Datawrapper's colour matching – the whole category vanishes from the legend with no error, and every row in it falls back to the default colour. Move the detail into a note; keep the category label plain.
  • A header row that's itself a number (e.g. a stray data value sitting where "Year" should be) means the file has no real header row – its first data row is being read as column names and silently dropped. Check row one.

Datawrapper has no beeswarm/packed-scatter type. If the question genuinely needs one, the layout can be precomputed and fed in as an ordinary scatter plot – ask CER; this is a real, working technique, not a workaround to avoid.

UNCTAD does not publish Datawrapper maps on unctad.org – the boundaries don't follow UN cartographic conventions. A map-shaped question needs a different tool.
Part 3

Colour

Colour should carry meaning, not decorate. The UNCTAD palette is assigned in a fixed order so the same category reads the same colour across every chart.

  • Four series is the target; seven is the hard limit. More than seven coloured series on one chart — stop and either group the tail into "Other" or split into two charts.
  • A series named Other, Rest of world, or All other … always gets warm grey and moves to the end of the legend, regardless of where it sits in the data.
  • Red is reserved for highlighting and annotation lines – never assign it to an ordinary series.
  • Country groups get a fixed, separate mapping, not the plain sequence above: Developed → UN blue, Developing → UN yellow, SIDS → UN dark blue, LDCs → UN dark yellow. Always this pairing, whichever groups a chart actually shows.
too many series
grouped
Part 4

Title

The title states the finding. Everything else on the chart supports it.

  • A sentence with a verb, not a subject label. "FDI inflows rose in 15 of 21 member states" states a finding; "FDI inflows by country" states a topic.
  • Never starts with print figure numbering – "Figure 7", "Fig. 2", "Chart 3", "Table 1" are references to a page layout that doesn't exist on the web.
  • No trailing full stop.
  • Under about 90 characters. Three lines on a phone screen is fine; four is not.
topic, not finding

"Foreign direct investment in ASEAN, 2021–2025"

states the finding

"Foreign direct investment in ASEAN reaches a record 246 billion dollars"

Part 5

Description

Four elements, comma-separated, always in this order.

what the data is, the breakdown, the unit, the period
Vegetable oil production, by crop, millions of metric tons, 1961–2023
  • Brackets are for abbreviations only – use commas for everything else.
  • No trailing full stop.
  • En dash for year ranges: 2010–2024, never 2010-2024.
  • Spell an abbreviation out on first use: "Gross domestic product (GDP) per capita", not a bare "GDP" with no expansion anywhere on the chart.
  • A bracketed unit shorthand reads awkwardly in prose – "by volume (kg) and value (USD)" should read "by volume in kilograms and value in dollars". A genuine citation parenthetical, like "UN Trade and Development (UNCTAD)", is not this case.
Part 6

Source and notes

The source line always names UNCTAD in full, and always the same way.

WhenTemplate
UNCTAD's own dataUN Trade and Development (UNCTAD).
Someone else's, reproducedUN Trade and Development (UNCTAD) based on [source].
UNCTAD computed the figuresUN Trade and Development (UNCTAD) calculations based on [source].
UNCTAD modelled or imputedUN Trade and Development (UNCTAD) estimates based on [source].
  • Never "UNCTAD" alone, never the French form on an English page.
  • The source line is the only field that ends with a full stop, and only when it's a genuine "based on …" sentence.
  • Name the database where one exists: "based on UNCTADstat", "based on the FDI/MNE database".
  • Fill the source URL field only when a real public landing page exists for it.
  • Notes carry methodology detail and definitions the source line and description have no room for – multiple separate facts read better each on their own line than run together in one paragraph.
Part 7

Lines, axes and labels

Axes should show the data honestly; labels should need no manual tuning.

truncated axis
zero baseline
two vertical axes
indexed to one base
empty headroom
fit to the data
  • Bars and columns start at zero. The rare exception (a measure with no natural zero, e.g. an index or an average age) must be stated in the notes, and a line chart should be considered first.
  • Never two vertical axes on one chart – use two charts, or index both series to a common base instead.
  • Axis tick labels stay on automatic. Hand-set custom ticks are a print-layout habit that doesn't travel – annotate a specific point instead of overriding the whole axis.
  • Vertical axis labels sit inside the plot area, not floating outside it.

Part 9

Common corrections

What CER's review changes most often – fix these yourself and review has almost nothing left to do.

  • Locale left on a Datawrapper default instead of en-CH.
  • Axis labels left on automatic placement instead of set to Inside.
  • Source line phrased differently from the templates in Part 6 – almost every chart has its own variant; there are only four correct shapes.
  • Palette picked by eye in the colour dialog instead of assigned in the documented order.
  • A title that names the subject ("FDI by country") instead of the finding ("FDI rose in most member states").
  • An em dash somewhere in the text fields – house style is the en dash, everywhere, every field.
  • A hyphen used for a year range ("2010-2024") instead of an en dash ("2010–2024").
  • Custom tick values left over from a print layout, now showing stale or misaligned labels on the web version.
  • A category label still carrying a bracketed threshold like "(<$100k)" – move it to the notes before it silently breaks the legend.
